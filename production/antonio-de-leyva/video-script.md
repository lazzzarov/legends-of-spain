# Video script — Antonio de Leyva: "The Winter at Pavia"

- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.
- **Look:** painted chronicle (animated oil painting and engraving, not photoreal). See `docs/PLAN_V2.md`.
- **Disclosure (required):** "AI-generated historical dramatization" on screen for the first 3 s and on the end card.

## Hook

A king is at the gates, the garrison is hungry and its mercenaries have not been paid. A sick commander has to keep them together through a whole winter until help comes.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Leyva:** veteran commander aged forty-four (winter 1524–25), born in 1480. Greying beard, lined face, visibly unwell on the day of the battle. Dark padded doublet and a plain breastplate of the 1520s under a heavy fur-lined gown, a flat cap. Carried in a plain wooden chair on 24 February. No plumed helmet, no heroic charge. His age and his illness that day are documented; his build, expression and exact dress are interpretation.
- **Likeness:** an engraved portrait exists (Aliprando Caprioli, 1596–1600; `portrait.jpg` in the legend file), made some sixty years after his death. The likeness may be guided by it. The result is always labelled an AI interpretation.
- **The garrison:** largely German landsknechts according to the ACAMI paper (`acami-pavia`), with Spaniards and Italians. Show them as different contingents, hungry and unpaid. Do not show an all-Spanish garrison.
- **The townspeople of Pavia:** present in the siege scenes. Soldiers were billeted on them and their churches' silver paid the wages (`rah-leiva`, `acami-pavia`).
- **Francis I and the French:** shown at a distance, without caricature.

## Do not show

- French soldiers drowning in the Ticino, or the dead on the field.
- The capture of Francis I by a named soldier; the sources disagree on who took him, and the legend file leaves it out.
- A purely Spanish victory; the legend file marks this as legend. Pescara, Lannoy, Bourbon and Frundsberg's landsknechts all had a part.
- Charles V enlisting as a soldier of Leyva's tercio; the legend file marks this story as legend.
- Leyva leading a charge on horseback; he was carried to the gate in a chair.
- Anachronisms: comb morions and the dress of the later tercios, uniforms, the red-and-yellow Spanish flag, star-shaped bastions.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- 24 February 1525, the battle (`rah-leiva`, `acami-pavia`, `britannica-pavia`).
- Four months of siege, from late October 1524. No exact start day: sources give 25 or 28 October.
- No figure for the garrison: sources range from 800 to 7,000.
- No figure for the French army (Britannica gives both 20,000 and 28,000), and no casualty figures.
