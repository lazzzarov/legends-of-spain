# Video script — Bernardino de Sahagún: "The Twelve Books"

- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.
- **Look:** painted chronicle (animated oil painting and engraving, not photoreal). See `docs/PLAN_V2.md`.
- **Disclosure (required):** "AI-generated historical dramatization" on screen for the first 3 s and on the end card.

## Hook

In a college at Tlatelolco, Nahua scholars and painters and an ageing friar are finishing twelve books about the Nahua world. A royal order has just demanded that the manuscripts be collected, and an epidemic is passing outside.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Sahagún:** a Franciscan friar born around 1499. About thirty-seven when he begins teaching at Tlatelolco in 1536 (beats 2 and 3); in his mid to late seventies in 1573–1577 (beats 1, 4, 5 and 6), thin, stooped, white-haired. A plain Franciscan habit with a knotted cord and sandals; no bishop's dress, no rich cloth. Check the habit's colour for Franciscans in 16th-century New Spain before the first still. His order and ages are documented; his face and manner are interpretation.
- **Likeness:** no portrait made in his lifetime is known. The legend file's image is a photograph of a later, undated lithograph, an imagined likeness. The face may be guided by it, but the result is always labelled an AI interpretation.
- **The Nahua scholars:** Antonio Valeriano, Alonso Vegerano, Martín Jacobita and Pedro de San Buenaventura are named as grammarians, and Diego de Grado, Bonifacio Maximiliano and Mateo Severino as scribes (`getty-dfc-richter`, `getty-competing-narratives`). No verified likeness exists for any of them; their dress is interpretation and should be checked against the codex's own paintings.
- **The Nahua artists:** many, most of them anonymous; Getty researchers distinguish at least nineteen artists' hands. Show them as skilled painters at work, never as background figures.
- **The codex:** twelve books in parallel columns of Nahuatl and Spanish with small paintings, bound today in three volumes in Florence. While it is being made, show loose gatherings, not bound books.

## Do not show

- Epidemic deaths, bodies or burials; the epidemic is shown only through absence.
- Scenes of the conquest, or the atrocities painted in Book 12.
- Human sacrifice or any sensational image of Nahua religion.
- The breaking or burning of sacred images. Sahagún did destroy some, but it is not staged here.
- Soldiers seizing the manuscripts, or a bonfire of books. The sources describe an order to collect them and papers scattered among the order's houses.
- Sahagún writing alone by candlelight as the single author.
- Anachronisms: printed books in piles, baroque architecture, Maya or Inca imagery, invented pages of the codex.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- 1529, 1536, 1547, 1573 and 1577 (`rah-sahagun`).
- 1575–1577 for the completion of the twelve books (`getty-dfc-richter`).
- Twelve books (`getty-dfc-richter`, `getty-project`).
- Supported but not used: some 2,500 paintings (the exact count is 2,472 images and decorative elements), and seven collaborators known by name.
- No epidemic death toll; the legend file says only millions.
