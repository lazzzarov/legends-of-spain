# Video script — Francisco Javier Balmis: "Arm to Arm"

- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.
- **Look:** painted chronicle (animated oil painting and engraving, not photoreal). See `docs/PLAN_V2.md`.
- **Disclosure (required):** "AI-generated historical dramatization" on screen for the first 3 s and on the end card.

## Hook

A vaccine exists, but it dies on long voyages. A surgeon proposes to carry it alive across two oceans, in the arms of children who cannot say no. If the chain breaks once, the voyage is for nothing.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Balmis:** military surgeon and physician from Alicante, aged 49 when the expedition sailed and 52 when he returned (born December 1753). Lean, greying hair tied back, clean-shaven. Dress of a Spanish professional man of about 1803: dark tailcoat, waistcoat, white neckcloth, breeches and stockings, dark cloak at sea. No white coat, no modern medical dress.
- **Likeness:** the legend file has a portrait (`portrait.jpg`, an engraving dated about 1800 whose provenance is not yet verified). The likeness may be guided by it, but the result is an AI interpretation and is always labelled as one.
- **Documented and interpretation:** the dates, the route, the numbers of children and the method are documented. Faces, gestures and the look of each scene are interpretation.
- **Isabel Zendal:** rector of the A Coruña foundling home, who cared for the children at every stage; her adoptive son was among them. Plain dark dress, shawl and white cap of a working woman of Galicia about 1803. The legend file gives no portrait of her, so her face is an interpretation. She appears in beats 3 and 4 at least.
- **The children:** 22 boys aged three to nine from foundling institutions in A Coruña and Santiago de Compostela; later 26 Mexican children. Plain institutional clothes. They are shown as children, never as cheerful volunteers.
- **José Salvany:** deputy who led the southern team; shown once, at the parting in Caracas, at a distance.

## Do not show

- Vaccination in close-up: no cutting, no pustules, no crying child held down.
- Smallpox victims, or sick or dying children. Two Mexican children died before they could return; this is not shown.
- The children as willing heroes, smiling or saluting. Nobody asked their consent.
- Balmis alone on a prow or lit as a lone saviour; Zendal and the children share the frame.
- Anachronisms: syringes and hypodermic needles, white coats, face masks, steamships, the red-and-yellow flag used as a civil flag on land.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- 22 boys aged three to nine left A Coruña on 30 November 1803 (`tuells-duro-2015`, `rah-balmis`).
- 26 Mexican children crossed the Pacific; Manila on 15 April 1805 (`tuells-duro-2015`).
- The split at Caracas on 8 May 1804; Salvany's death in Cochabamba in 1810 (`rah-salvany`).
- Only one child still carrying live vaccine near Venezuela (`tuells-duro-2015`).
- No figure for people vaccinated or lives saved: the legend file gives none.
- No figure for the enslaved women bought in Havana: the sources give three or four, and the episode is not narrated here.
