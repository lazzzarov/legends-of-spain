- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.
- **Look:** painted chronicle (animated oil painting and engraving, not photoreal). See `docs/PLAN_V2.md`.

## Hook

Fewer than two hundred men hide around a square and wait for an emperor who has just won a civil war. What happens next is an ambush on people who do not fight back, and the film says so.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Pizarro:** soldier from Trujillo in his mid-fifties at Cajamarca (born c. 1475–1478, so the exact age is uncertain). Grey beard, lean, scarred; the legend file records that he was wounded seven times on the earlier voyages. Plain dark doublet, a worn steel breastplate and an open helmet of the 1520s or 1530s, sword. A veteran of thirty years in the Indies, not a courtier: no plumed hat, no rich cloak. His origin, illiteracy and long service are documented; his face, build and exact dress are interpretation.
- **Likeness:** no portrait from life is known. The portrait in the legend file (`portrait.jpg`, Amable-Paul Coutan, 1834–1835) is itself an imagined likeness. The film's likeness may be guided by it for continuity with the site. The result is always labelled an AI interpretation.
- **Atahualpa:** a ruler fresh from victory in a war of succession, carried on a gilded litter. Calm and commanding. Royal Inca dress checked against a source; no likeness from life is known, so his appearance is interpretation.
- **The retinue:** thousands of attendants and the lords who carry the litter. By the Spaniards' own account they did not fight back (`xerez-relacion`). Never show them armed or attacking.
- **Andean allies:** peoples such as the Huancas and Chachapoyas sided with the Spaniards against Cuzco (`rah-pizarro`, `maccormack-atahualpa`). Show them as distinct from the Inca court and from each other.

## Do not show

- The killing in the square. The film shows the moment before and the empty litter after, and calls it a massacre, not a battle.
- The garrotting of Atahualpa, the execution of Almagro or the killing of Pizarro.
- The line drawn in the sand on the island of Gallo; the legend file marks it as disputed.
- Words spoken by Valverde or Atahualpa, or Atahualpa throwing the book in anger; the legend file says only that he let it fall.
- A handful of Spaniards conquering an empire alone; the legend file marks this as legend.
- Smallpox scenes; the diagnosis of the epidemic of the late 1520s is disputed.
- Anachronisms: comb morions and later armour, uniforms, the red-and-yellow Spanish flag, Mexica or generic feathered costume on Andean people, coins in the ransom room.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- 16 November 1532, July 1533 and 1541 (`rah-pizarro`).
- Fewer than two hundred Spaniards at Cajamarca (`rah-pizarro`, `maccormack-atahualpa`). No exact count: accounts differ between 160 and 200.
- Some two thousand killed, only as the estimate of Pizarro's secretary (`xerez-relacion`). No independent count exists.
- Thousands of attendants, with no figure.
- No figure for the ransom or for the later collapse of the Indigenous population.
