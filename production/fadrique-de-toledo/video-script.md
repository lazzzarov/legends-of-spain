## Hook

A colonial capital falls in a day. Two kingdoms that share a king but not their laws must sail one fleet across the Atlantic to take it back.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Fadrique de Toledo:** captain general of the Armada of the Ocean Sea. By the Real Academia de la Historia's date (born 1580) he was 44 at Bahia; a more recent study argues for a birth about 1589, which would make him about 36. Paint him as a man of about forty. Dark moustache and pointed beard. Dress of a Spanish general of the 1620s: doublet and breeches, stiff flat golilla collar, steel gorget, red general's sash, baton, boots, broad-brimmed hat. No full armour.
- **Likeness:** the legend file has a portrait (`portrait.jpg`, a detail of Juan Bautista Maíno's painting, finished after his death). The likeness may be guided by it, but the result is an AI interpretation and is always labelled as one. Do not copy Maíno's composition.
- **Documented and interpretation:** his command, the dates and the surrender are documented. His age is disputed. His gestures and where he stood on 30 April and 1 May are interpretation.
- **The armada:** Castilian, Portuguese and Neapolitan soldiers and sailors, told apart by their flags. Never show a single-nation force.
- **Manuel de Meneses:** commander of the Portuguese squadron; shown on his own flagship in beat 3. No likeness is given in the legend file, so keep him at a distance.
- **The people of Bahia:** enslaved Africans working the sugar colony are shown with dignity and as part of the story, not as scenery.

## Do not show

- Maíno's tapestry scene, or any staged act of submission; the Prado notes it is not in the accounts.
- The sack of the city or the removal of church silver; these are later Portuguese accusations and are not narrated here.
- The execution of Francisco, leader of the African company armed by the Dutch, or any other execution.
- Graphic violence against enslaved people, or battle deaths in close-up.
- Anachronisms: 18th-century ships of the line, tricorns and uniform coats, the red-and-yellow Spanish flag, later buildings in Salvador.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- 74 days at sea, from Cádiz on 14 January to the bay on 29 March 1625 (`brasilhis-fadrique-de-toledo`).
- Surrender on 30 April and entry on 1 May 1625 (`brasilhis-fadrique-de-toledo`, `rah-fadrique-de-toledo`).
- No single figure for the fleet or the troops: the sources give 52 to 62 ships and about 12,000 to 12,600 men. If a figure is ever needed, give the range.
- The count of prisoners, ships, guns and flags comes from one source (`rah-fadrique-de-toledo`) and is not used.
