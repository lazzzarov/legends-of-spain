## Disputed points and how they were handled

- **Date of death.** The RAH gives 6 August 1539 (dusk), with the last defenders of the sea fort surrendering at 4 a.m. on 7 August. The Alcaraz–Tapia relation and Sandoval (via González Castrillo) place the fall of the castle and Sarmiento's death on Thursday 7 August. The file uses `display: '6 or 7 August 1539'`, and the chapter says "by 7 August".
- **Start of the siege.** The RAH says besieged from 12 July. The relation has Barbarossa arriving on 15 July. Another eyewitness (via González Castrillo) says 17 July, and Sepúlveda 18 July. The chapters say "mid-July".
- **Garrison size.** Sandoval gives 3,000 or 2,500; Rocca nearly 4,000; Antonio Doria 3,000; the slave narrator fewer than 3,000 at the start; Haedo 4,000; _Viaje de Turquía_ 3,500. The RAH describes 12 companies of 300 men (3,600 nominal) plus 85 stradiots. The relation speaks of 15 banners. Given as a 2,500–4,000 range, `disputed`.
- **Ottoman strength.** Doria's report gives 18,000 + 4,000 janissaries. Sandoval gives 30,000 by land plus 10,000 Turks and 4,000 janissaries aboard. Alcaraz–Tapia give 20,000 aboard plus 130,000 by land. Given as a 22,000–150,000 range, `disputed`. The relation's figures are self-serving, as Floristán notes.
- **Ottoman losses.** The corporals give 40,000 (the Turks themselves said 20,000–25,000). Sandoval gives 17,000 or 37,000; Doria about 3,000. Mentioned only in otherSide, as an example of inflation.
- **Survivors.** About 400 captives (relation); about 800 including women and boys (Sandoval); about 200 soldiers escaped (Balarino report, 23 Aug 1539). Given as 200–800, `disputed`.
- **The defiant reply.** Popular retellings attribute a pithy "let them come when they will" to Sarmiento. The only near-contemporary wording found is the corporals' reported speech of a collective decision. It is treated as a `legend` myth, and the comic uses the sourced wording as a translated quotation with the Spanish original in the panel note.
- **Second corporal's first name.** Fernández Álvarez (via González Castrillo) calls him "Francisco de Tapia". The Alba copy edited by Floristán reads "Juan de Tapia". The edition is followed.

- Check Floristán's further source, L. Gil, "El triste galardón del heroísmo: Castilnuovo (1539)", _Erytheia_ 26 (2005), on the names of ransomed captives.

- Ottoman-side sources (the dossier mentions them) were not consulted.

## Portrait candidates

No portrait of Sarmiento, contemporary or later, was found. The RAH entry has none, and a Wikimedia Commons search for "Francisco Sarmiento Castelnuovo" returned nothing relevant. The file uses an honest placeholder ("No contemporary portrait known", licence `[To verify]`). Recommendation: commission an `original-illustration` clearly labelled as imaginative, or use a period view of Castelnuovo instead of a face. Never use an AI image as the portrait. If a period print of Castelnuovo is used, remember that under Spanish LPI art. 128, non-creative photographs of artworks are protected for 25 years. Prefer scans released under CC0 or as public domain by the holding institution.
