# Video script — Álvaro de Bazán: "From the Sea"

- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.
- **Look:** painted chronicle (animated oil painting and engraving, not photoreal). See `docs/PLAN_V2.md`.
- **Disclosure (required):** "AI-generated historical dramatization" on screen for the first 3 s and on the end card.

## Hook

An admiral must take an island stronghold that no fleet alone can capture. He wins the sea fight, then comes back with a new kind of boat built to put soldiers on a defended beach. The victory has a cost, and the script states it plainly.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Bazán:** the marquis of Santa Cruz, aged fifty-five at the battle of 1582 and fifty-six at the landing of 1583 (born 12 December 1526). Grey hair and a trimmed grey beard, upright and unhurried. On deck: a dark breastplate over a dark doublet, a small ruff, a plain sash of command. Ashore: black doublet and short cape. No full tournament armour, no 18th-century naval coat. His rank and age are documented; his build and expression are interpretation.
- **Likeness:** the legend file's image is a posthumous engraving of 1759–1802 by Bartolomé Vázquez after Antonio Carnicero, whose plate names the first marquis; it was not made from life. The face may be guided by it, but the result is always labelled an AI interpretation. Do not use van Dyck's portrait, which shows his son.
- **The ships:** sailing galleons and naos of the 1580s for the Atlantic battle; oared galleys and flat-bottomed barges with ramp bows for the landing. The galleys tow the barges.
- **The troops:** Spanish, German and Italian soldiers (`rgm-gonzalez-molina`): morions or brimmed hats, breastplates over plain clothes, pikes and arquebuses. No single national uniform.
- **The other side:** Filippo Strozzi's French fleet in 1582; on Terceira, Portuguese islanders loyal to Dom António and their French allies. Show them as soldiers and townspeople, not as villains.
- **The oarsmen:** forced labour at the oars (`rah-bazan`). Present whenever a galley is shown close up.

## Do not show

- The executions at Vila Franca: no beheadings, hangings, scaffolds or bodies. They are stated in the voiceover and never staged.
- Close-up killing in either battle, drowning men, or any sack of Angra.
- People being enslaved on Terceira and Faial; the only source in the legend file is a celebratory family memorial.
- Anything that suggests the Armada of 1588 would have succeeded under Bazán. That is speculation.
- Anachronistic ships and kit: ships of the line, 18th-century uniforms and tricorns, modern steel landing craft.
- Anachronistic flags: no red-and-yellow Spanish ensign and no French tricolour, both of which are later. Check period flags against Museo Naval material, or show none.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- 26 July 1582, and some five hours of fighting (`rah-bazan`).
- 26 July 1583 for the landing, following the Revista General de Marina; the Real Academia de la Historia gives 23 July for the surrender. If the editor prefers, say July 1583.
- 1588 for his death (`rah-bazan`).
- No fleet sizes for 1582 (about 25 against 30 to more than 60 is disputed) and none for 1583 (98 ships, more than 8,000 soldiers and seven barges come from one source). Say only that the French fleet was larger.
- No count of those executed in the voiceover.
