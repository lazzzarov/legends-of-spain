# Video script — Hernán Cortés: "Many Hands"

- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.
- **Look:** painted chronicle (animated oil painting and engraving, not photoreal). See `docs/PLAN_V2.md`.
- **Disclosure (required):** "AI-generated historical dramatization" on screen for the first 3 s and on the end card.

## Hook

A beaten force limps into an allied city after a night flight, and the city's rulers decide whether it eats. The war that follows is won by a coalition, an interpreter and an epidemic, not by one captain.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Cortés:** from Extremadura, aged thirty-four to thirty-six (1519–1521). Dark hair and beard. Dark wool doublet and hose, a plain steel breastplate and an open helmet or a flat cap of the 1510s, sword at the hip. A private captain, not a royal general: no plumes, no full plate, no later comb morion. His birth year, origin and age are documented; his build, expression and exact dress are interpretation.
- **Likeness:** a drawing made in his lifetime exists (Christoph Weiditz, 1529; `portrait.jpg` in the legend file). The likeness may be guided by it, allowing that it shows him eight to ten years older. The result is always labelled an AI interpretation.
- **Malintzin:** a young Nahua woman, enslaved as a girl and handed to the Spaniards by the lords of Tabasco. Hair worn long, a woven huipil and skirt. She stands between speakers and is shown talking. No portrait from life is known; her appearance is interpretation.
- **Tlaxcalan rulers and allies:** shown as decision-makers and as the larger part of every army. Tlaxcalans, Texcocans, Chalcas and Otomís are separate peoples; check dress and insignia against a source and do not give them one generic costume.
- **The Mexica:** defenders of their own city, shown with the same dignity as everyone else.

## Do not show

- The killings at Cholula and at the festival of Toxcatl. Both are named in the voiceover and shown only as aftermath, without bodies.
- Deaths on the causeways, smallpox victims in close-up, or the destruction of the city as spectacle.
- The death of Moctezuma (the circumstances are disputed) and the execution of Cuauhtémoc.
- Cortés received as the god Quetzalcoatl; the legend file marks this as disputed.
- A few hundred Spaniards winning alone; the legend file marks this as legend. Every army scene shows the coalition.
- Burning or scuttled ships, and any scene of Cortés speaking to his men: neither is in the legend file.
- Anachronisms: comb morions and full plate armour, uniforms, the red-and-yellow Spanish flag, ocean galleons on the lake, horses or steel on the Mexica side.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- February 1519, the sailing from Cuba; 13 August 1521, the fall of Tenochtitlan (`rah-cortes`).
- Thirteen brigantines, built in Tlaxcala (`rah-cortes`).
- Thousands killed at Cholula, with no figure (`rah-cortes`, `navarrete-guerra`).
- No figure for the Indigenous coalition: sources range from 25,000 to 200,000 and the legend file marks it as disputed.
- No figure for smallpox deaths (5–8 million is one disputed estimate), and no figure for losses on the night flight.
