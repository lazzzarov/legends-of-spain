## Disputed points and how they were handled

- **Birth year.** RAH gives 12 June 1502 and the Basque government "hacia 1502"; Britannica, EBSCO and older works say c. 1510 (seen in search results, not opened). The placeholder had 1510. The file now uses `{ year: 1502, approx: true }` with a `disputed` fact "c. 1502–1510".
- **Cebu, April 1565.** The burning is in the contemporary Relation, which says it was caused accidentally or by the islanders. The text says "about a hundred houses burned" without assigning blame.
- **When the Santo Niño was found.** B&R records 28 April 1565. RAH links the find to the 1567 renaming of the town. The comic uses 1565 (B&R).
- **Tone of the sources.** The RAH entry is sympathetic and stresses diplomacy. The 1903 B&R editorial summaries use prejudiced language ("knaves", "treachery"). Neither tone is reproduced. The `otherSide` names this directly.
- **"Peaceful conquest".** Shown as a myth (`legend`), with the documented burnings.

## Open questions

- No modern Filipino scholarship was opened in this pass. Recommended: W. H. Scott, _Barangay_ and _Looking for the Prehispanic Filipino_; NHCP material on the Battle of Bangkusay (3 June 1571), which RAH does not mention. Inquirer reports of the NHCP's 2016 marker to "the young leader of Macabebe" were blocked (HTTP 403). The battle was left out until a source can be opened.
- The date of the Cebu treaty (usually given as 4 June 1565) needs a source that was actually opened.
- The archive (PARES) has the requerimientos (PATRONATO,24,R.6) and autos (R.7) of 1568. Consult the images for the exchange with Pereira.
- The claim (from the dossier) that the route linked American silver with Chinese goods was left out because no opened source stated it. A trade-history source would let it be added.
