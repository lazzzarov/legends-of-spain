# Video script — Miguel López de Legazpi: "The Sealed Orders"

- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.
- **Look:** painted chronicle (animated oil painting and engraving, not photoreal). See `docs/PLAN_V2.md`.
- **Disclosure (required):** "AI-generated historical dramatization" on screen for the first 3 s and on the end card.

## Hook

An ageing official from Mexico City opens sealed orders at sea. He must hold on in islands whose people have good reason to distrust him, until one ship finds the way home that no ship has found before.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Legazpi:** an official from Mexico City, about sixty in 1564 and nearly seventy in 1571 if born c. 1502 (the birth year is disputed, c. 1502–1510). Grey beard, a lawyer's bearing rather than a soldier's. A dark wool doublet and gown, a small plain ruff, a dark flat cap; a breastplate only where troops are landing. No plumed helmet, no conquistador pose. His career as a notary and official is documented; his appearance is interpretation.
- **Likeness:** no portrait from life is known. The legend file's image is an 1887 wood engraving, an imagined likeness. The face may be guided by it, but the result is always labelled an AI interpretation.
- **Andrés de Urdaneta:** a friar and the expedition's navigator. A friar's habit, weathered hands, a chart or instrument. He is the real navigator of the return voyage (`rah-urdaneta`). His age is not in the legend file; check it before the first still.
- **Felipe de Salcedo:** Legazpi's grandson, in command of the San Pedro on the return. Show him as a young man; his exact age is not in the legend file.
- **The fleet:** two galleons and two smaller pataches (`blair-robertson-v2`). The San Pedro is the ship on which the orders were opened and the ship that sailed back.
- **The people of the islands:** the people of Cebu under Tupas; at Maynila, Rajah Matanda, Rajah Soliman and Lakandula, with Chinese and Japanese traders living in the town. Cebuano and Panayan fighters in their own boats sailed with the Spaniards. Their dress is interpretation and must be checked against Philippine sources before the first still.

## Do not show

- The burning of Cebu's houses or of Maynila in close-up, or any killing. The legend file does not say who started the Cebu fire, so no one is shown setting it.
- The sacking of villages on Guam; it is not in this script.
- Islanders as treacherous or as a faceless crowd. The Spanish reports and their 1903 editors use that tone; the script does not.
- The three rulers kneeling or handing over the town as a gift.
- Legazpi aboard the returning San Pedro, or holding navigation instruments as if he found the route.
- Anachronisms: stone-walled Manila, baroque churches, 18th-century ships and uniforms, later flags.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- Four days out of Navidad, November 1564 (`rah-legazpi`).
- About a hundred houses burned at Cebu (`blair-robertson-v2`).
- 1 June and 8 October 1565 for the return voyage (`rah-urdaneta`).
- June 1571 for the founding of Spanish Manila; the route in use until 1815 (`rah-legazpi`).
- Supported but not used: four ships; sixteen deaths on the return voyage.
- No crew total: about three hundred Spaniards was the viceroy's plan, not a count. No birth year: it is disputed.
