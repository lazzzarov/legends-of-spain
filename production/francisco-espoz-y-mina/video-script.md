- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.
- **Length:** 60 s target (45–90 s allowed) · 16:9 master, 9:16 social cut.
- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.

## Hook

A guerrilla band loses its leader in a dawn raid. A farmer who has never commanded anyone is chosen by six survivors, and builds an army with its own taxes and courts. The order he imposes has a price.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Espoz y Mina:** Navarrese farmer turned guerrilla commander, aged 28 at Labiano in 1810, 29 at the Arlabán pass and 33 at Pamplona in 1814 (born June 1781). Sturdy, dark hair, side whiskers. In 1810: country clothes of Navarre, short dark jacket, sash, breeches, cloth leggings, rope-soled sandals, cloak, musket. From the Arlabán pass onwards: a plain dark military coat with few ornaments. No gold-laced general's uniform before 1814.
- **Likeness:** the legend file has a portrait (`portrait.jpg`, an anonymous oil dated about 1815–1823; the date and holding museum are not yet confirmed). The likeness may be guided by it, but the result is an AI interpretation and is always labelled as one.
- **Documented and interpretation:** the dates, the six men, the customs posts and courts, and the Arlabán ambush are documented, largely from his own service record and memoirs through `rah-espoz-y-mina`. His dress in each year, his face and his gestures are interpretation.
- **Javier Mina:** his young nephew, the band's first leader; shown only at a distance in beat 1, being led away.
- **The guerrillas:** countrymen in mixed civilian dress in 1810; drilled and organised in battalions by 1811. Any uniform is an interpretation.
- **Villagers, priests and mayors:** shown as people under pressure from both sides, not as cheering supporters.

## Do not show

- Executions of any kind: the shooting of the rival chief Echeverría, hanged prisoners, hostages, or the later reprisals in Catalonia and Navarre.
- The killing at the Arlabán pass. The still stops before the volley.
- A whole people rising in arms; the legend file treats that as a myth.
- Guerrillas winning a pitched battle against French regulars.
- Anachronisms: red Carlist berets and other dress of the 1830s, percussion rifles, later French uniforms with kepis, the red-and-yellow flag as an army colour.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- Six men at Labiano on 29 March 1810; some 400 more soon after (`rah-espoz-y-mina`).
- Arlabán pass, 25 May 1811: one cartridge each; around 1,000 Spanish and British prisoners freed (`rah-espoz-y-mina`).
- The escort of about 1,200, of whom some 400 got back to Vitoria, is in the legend file but is not narrated, to avoid a body count.
- No figure for the size of his force (3,000 to 11,000 are his own figures) and no figure for French casualties (the 40,000 he claimed is disputed).
