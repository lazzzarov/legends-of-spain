- **Status:** draft, awaiting the editor's approval. No footage is generated before approval.

- **Voice:** English, third person, one narrator for the whole series. No invented dialogue.

## Hook

A boat party sent downstream to find food for a starving expedition cannot row back against the current. The only way left is onward, down a river whose peoples have never seen them before.

## Character sheet

Use the same description in every prompt.

- **Orellana:** a man of about thirty (born c. 1511; events of 1541–1542), from Trujillo in Extremadura. He had lost an eye in the conquest of Peru (`rah-orellana`); the legend file does not say which, so settle it before the first still and keep it the same in every shot. Dark beard, thin and sunburnt. A worn linen shirt, a faded padded doublet, loose breeches, a plain sword belt. No plate armour, no crested morion, no plumed hat. The lost eye is documented; the rest of his appearance is interpretation.
- **Likeness:** no portrait from life is known. The legend file's image is a photograph of a modern bust in Trujillo, an imagined likeness. The face may be guided by it, but the result is always labelled an AI interpretation.
- **The boats:** a small brigantine, the San Pedro, built on the Coca by a starving company, and later a second, sturdier one built in Aparia's lands; open river boats with oars and a simple sail, plus dugout canoes. Not seagoing ships.
- **Gaspar de Carvajal:** the friar who chronicled the journey. He lost an eye to an arrow (`rah-carvajal`); show a bandage in beat 6 only, never the wound. Check his order's habit before the first still.
- **The company:** Spaniards in ragged working clothes and two Black men whose names were not recorded (`rah-orellana`). Do not show an all-European crew.
- **The peoples of the river:** many, in large settlements, as Carvajal described and archaeology now supports (`uea-da-silva`, `de-souza-2018`). Their dress and houses are interpretation; keep them plain and do not borrow from other cultures.

## Do not show

- Battles in close-up, wounds, deaths, or the taking of villages; the fighting is stated, never staged.
- Women warriors. Carvajal's story of them is his claim, not established fact.
- Any scene that settles the treason question: no plotting, no vow to return, no abandoned men cursing the boat. Historians still disagree.
- Orellana planting a flag or cross as a discoverer; the river was already home to millions.
- Anachronistic kit: full plate armour, morion helmets on every head, large galleons on the river, later uniforms or flags.
- An empty, untouched jungle. The banks were heavily populated.

## Numbers allowed in voiceover

- Fifty-seven men, given as the friar's count (`carvajal-medina-1894`); the Real Academia de la Historia gives fifty-six, so the number is never stated as a plain fact.
- Two hundred leagues without food (`carvajal-medina-1894`).
- 26 December 1541 and 26 August 1542 (`rah-gonzalo-pizarro`, `rah-orellana`, `rah-carvajal`).
- Eleven deaths on the river (`rah-orellana`).
- No figures for the second expedition, and no count of porters; the legend file says only thousands.
